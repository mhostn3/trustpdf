Hotels, landlords, letting agents, car rental firms, employers and online services often ask for a copy of your passport or ID card. Once you send it, you lose control of it: it can sit in an inbox for years, be leaked in a breach, or be reused to open accounts in your name. A simple watermark that says who the copy is for and when makes it much less useful to anyone else.

What a watermark does, and what it doesn't

  • It marks the purpose. A copy stamped "For ACME Lettings, rental application, November 2026" is hard to pass off for anything else, and many places check for exactly that kind of marking.
  • It shows where a leak came from. If a stamped copy turns up somewhere it shouldn't, you know who it was sent to.
  • It does not make the document secret. Your name, photo and number can still be read. That is the point: the recipient needs to read them.

1. Turn your photo or scan into a PDF

If you have a photo of your ID, open Images to PDF, add the front and back images, and create the PDF. If you already have a PDF scan, skip this step.

2. Add the watermark

  1. Open Watermark PDF and drop in the PDF.
  2. In Watermark text, write who it is for, why, and the date, for example Copy for ACME Lettings – rental application – 23/11/2026.
  3. Choose Diagonal, across the page so the text runs over the document itself, not only the margin.
  4. Set an opacity where the text is clearly visible but the photo and number can still be read, often around 25–40%.
  5. Click Add Watermark, save the file and open it to check that everything is still readable.

3. Make it harder to remove

The watermark is added as text on top of the page. Someone with a PDF editor and some effort could delete it. To bake it into the image, run the watermarked file through Compress PDF with the Strong setting, which redraws each page as a single image. Check that the ID is still legible afterwards.

Share as little as you can

  • Ask whether they really need a copy, or only need to see the original.
  • Some services only need the photo page of a passport, not every page.
  • Where the law and the recipient allow it, black out what they don't need, such as the document number or your signature, with Redact PDF. Many official bodies and banks do need the full document, so ask first.
  • Send it through the recipient's official upload page or a protected PDF rather than a chat app where it will stay forever.

Why do this offline?

Watermarking an ID on a website that uploads it defeats the purpose: you would be giving an unmarked copy to one more company. Watermark PDF, Images to PDF and Compress PDF run in your browser, so the image of your ID never leaves your device.
