"Attachment too large." It always happens with the one document that has to go out today: a scanned contract, a portfolio, a set of photos turned into a PDF. Here is how big an attachment can be, why your PDF is so large, and how to shrink it in a minute without uploading it to a compression website.

How big can an email attachment be?

  • Gmail: about 25 MB per email in total.
  • Yahoo Mail: about 25 MB.
  • Outlook.com: about 20 MB. Company Outlook and Exchange servers are often set lower.
  • Many company and government mail servers: 10 MB or less, and some reject anything over 5 MB.

Attachments are encoded for sending, which makes them roughly a third larger on the way, so a 20 MB PDF can count as about 27 MB. Aim for well under the limit; under 10 MB is safe almost everywhere.

Why is my PDF so big?

Text takes very little space: a hundred pages of plain text is often under 1 MB. Size comes almost entirely from images:

  • Scanned pages, especially scanned in colour at 300 DPI or more.
  • Photos taken with a phone and turned into a PDF at full camera resolution.
  • Pictures pasted into Word or PowerPoint without being resized.

So the fix is to store those images more efficiently, or at a resolution that suits a screen.

How to compress a PDF

  1. Open Compress PDF and drop the file into the box.
  2. Choose a level:
    • Lossless rewrites the file structure more efficiently. Nothing you see changes and text stays selectable. Try this first.
    • Strong (150 DPI) turns each page into a compressed image. Best for scans and photo-heavy files; still sharp on screen and in normal printing.
    • Maximum (100 DPI) gives the smallest file, fine for reading on screen.
  3. Click Compress PDF and look at the new size.
  4. If it is small enough, click Save Compressed PDF. If not, start again with a stronger level.

Strong and Maximum turn pages into images, so text can no longer be selected or searched. If that matters, run the compressed file through OCR PDF afterwards, which adds an invisible text layer and only a few kilobytes per page.

Still too big?

  • Send only what is needed. Take the relevant pages out with Split PDF or Extract PDF Pages.
  • Split it into parts and send two or three emails.
  • Rescan in black and white or greyscale at 200 DPI if you still have the paper. Colour scans of black-and-white documents waste a lot of space.
  • Share a link from your own cloud storage instead of attaching the file.

Why compress in your browser?

The documents people most often need to shrink, such as scanned IDs, contracts and medical records, are exactly the ones that should not be uploaded to a free compression website.
