Every PDF has a few fields that describe it: a title, an author, a subject and keywords. You rarely see them, but other people do. The title is what a browser tab shows when the PDF is opened online, what some PDF readers show in their title bar, and often what Google shows as the headline of a PDF in its results. The author is usually filled in automatically with your name, or with the name of whoever created the template you started from.

When it is worth changing

  • A wrong or embarrassing title. Files made from a copied document keep its old title, so a CV can show up as "Report Q3 final" in the reader's title bar.
  • Someone else's name as author. Templates, company laptops and second-hand files carry the original author's name.
  • PDFs you publish on a website. A clear title helps search engines and people choose your file. A blank title makes them fall back to the file name, such as "scan0042.pdf".
  • Merged files take the title and author of the first file you merged, which is rarely right for the whole thing.

Step 1: see the current values

Open the PDF Metadata Viewer, drop in your file and click View Metadata. It shows the title, author, subject, keywords, the programs that made the file, the dates, and whether there is a hidden XMP copy of the metadata.

Step 2: change them

  1. Open the PDF Metadata Editor and drop in the file. The current values are filled in for you.
  2. Type the new Title, Author, Subject and Keywords. Separate keywords with commas. Leave a field empty to remove it.
  3. You can also change Creator (the program the document was written in) and Producer (the software that made the PDF), or empty them.
  4. Tick Keep the original creation and modification dates if the dates should stay as they are. Otherwise the modification date is set to now.
  5. Click Update Metadata and then Save Updated PDF.

The pages themselves are not touched, and the file never leaves your device.

Why the old title can still show up

Many PDFs store their metadata twice: in the classic document properties and in a separate XMP block. Some readers show the XMP values first, so if you only change the classic fields, the old title or author can keep appearing. The editor's option Also remove the embedded XMP metadata stream removes that second copy, so the values you typed are the only ones left. Leave it ticked unless you know you need the XMP data, for example for a print workflow.

Writing a good title

  • Describe the document, not the file: "Annual Report 2026, Lisbon Rowing Club" rather than "AR_final_v3".
  • Keep it under about 60 characters so it is not cut off in search results and browser tabs.
  • For a CV or cover letter, use your name and the document type, for example "Ana Silva, CV".
  • Do not put anything in the title that you would not write on the first page; anyone who opens the file can read it.
