There are three ways to get a PDF job done without paying for a full editor: install a desktop program, use an online service that processes the file on its servers, or use a web tool that does the work in your browser. They look similar from the outside but handle your document very differently. This page compares them on the points that matter, without picking on any particular product.

How each one works

  • Desktop program: you download and install software, and it processes files on your computer.
  • Online (server-based) tool: you open a website, upload the file, the provider's server processes it, and you download the result from the server.
  • In-browser tool: you open a website, the page downloads the program, and your browser processes the file on your device. The file is not sent anywhere. TrustPDF works this way.

The privacy difference

With a server-based tool, a copy of your document exists on someone else's computer, at least for a while. Reputable providers delete files after a set time and protect their servers well, but you cannot check that. With a desktop program or an in-browser tool, the document stays with you. For anything personal or confidential, such as IDs, contracts, tax papers, medical or HR records, that is the safer default. "Runs in your browser" is still a claim. You can test it yourself in two minutes: {% guide_link 'check-pdf-tool-does-not-upload-files' "how to check that a PDF tool doesn't upload your files" %}.

Which one should you use?

  • You work with PDFs every day on one computer: a desktop program is worth installing.
  • The document is not sensitive and you need a heavy conversion: a reputable server-based service is fine.
  • The document is personal or confidential, you are on a phone, or you can't install software: an in-browser tool gives you the convenience of a website without handing over the file.

What TrustPDF does and doesn't do

TrustPDF covers common jobs, such as merging, compressing, signing, redacting, password protection and editing metadata, all in the browser and free. It does not edit the existing text of a PDF, and OCR and Word conversion run slower than on a server because your own device does the work. If you need full text editing every day, a desktop editor will serve you better; {% guide_link 'free-adobe-acrobat-alternatives' "free alternatives to Adobe Acrobat" %} lists options for each task.
