Accountants, banks, landlords, lenders and new employers regularly ask for a tax return, a payslip or a bank statement as a PDF. These files hold the details a fraudster most wants: your full name and address, tax or social security number, employer, salary and bank account. A few minutes of preparation make a big difference if the email is forwarded, stored in the wrong place or the recipient's mailbox is breached.

1. Check what is really needed

Ask, or read the request again. "Proof of income" is often satisfied by the summary page of a tax return or a single recent payslip, not the full 30-page return. Fewer pages means less to lose.

Keep only the pages you need with Extract PDF Pages, or delete the others with Remove PDF Pages.

2. Remove details the recipient doesn't need

A landlord checking your income does not need your bank account number, your tax identification number or your children's names. Black them out with Redact PDF: search for a number to remove it on every page, or drag over an area. Unlike a black box drawn in an ordinary editor, it removes the text from the file. {% guide_link 'redact-pdf-without-uploading' "How to redact a PDF properly" %} explains the difference and how to check the result.

Do not redact documents for your accountant, the tax office or a bank's formal process unless they agree: they may need the full document.

3. Check the hidden information

PDFs exported from payroll or tax software can carry metadata such as the software name, the company that generated them and sometimes a person's name. It is usually harmless, but if you redacted the document, clean the metadata too with the PDF Metadata Editor, as described in {% guide_link 'remove-metadata-from-pdf' "how to remove metadata from a PDF" %}.

4. Put a password on it

Ordinary email is not end-to-end encrypted, and attachments sit in at least two mailboxes, often for years. Encrypting the PDF itself protects it wherever it ends up.

  1. Open Protect PDF and drop in the file.
  2. Type a password to open the PDF, and repeat it. A long phrase of four or five random words is strong and easy to read out.
  3. Click Protect PDF and save. The file is encrypted with AES-256.

Send the password through a different channel from the file: email the PDF, then text the password or say it on the phone. Never put it in the same email. More in {% guide_link 'password-protect-pdf-free' "how to password protect a PDF" %}.

5. Send it to the right place

  • If the bank, tax office or employer has a secure upload portal, use that instead of email.
  • Double-check the recipient's address. Payment and document fraud often starts with a lookalike address or a reply-to that is not the real one.
  • Be suspicious of an unexpected request for these documents, even from a known name. Confirm it by phone using a number you already have.

Why not use an online tool for this?

Many free PDF sites upload the file to process it. For a tax return that means handing the unredacted, unencrypted original, the very thing you are trying to protect, to a third party first. Every tool in this guide runs in your browser, so the original never leaves your device. {% guide_link 'are-online-pdf-tools-safe' "Are online PDF tools safe?" %} goes into the details.
