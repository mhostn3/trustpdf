Visa applications, residence permits and rental applications all ask for the same kind of documents: a passport, payslips, bank statements, an employment contract, sometimes a tax return. They are also exactly the documents you should not upload to a free converter site just to get them into the right format. This guide shows how to prepare them on your own device.

Start with the requirements

Read the instructions of the embassy, the visa centre, the agency or the landlord before you begin, and note:

  • whether they want one PDF with everything or one file per document;
  • the maximum file size, often a few megabytes per file;
  • whether scans must be in colour and at a particular resolution;
  • any rules for file names.

Requirements differ a lot between countries and offices, so the official instructions always win.

1. Turn paper documents into PDFs

If you have photos or scans as JPG or PNG files, open Images to PDF, add the images in order, choose A4 (or US Letter) as the page size and create the PDF. A fixed page size makes the pages print and display consistently. Take photos flat, in daylight, with all four corners of the page visible.

2. Combine pages that belong together

Three months of payslips or bank statements usually arrive as three files. Combine them with Merge PDF, oldest first, so the reader sees one document per category. If the application wants a single file for everything, merge all the categories in the order of their checklist.

3. Send only what is asked for

  • Leave out pages that aren't needed. A bank statement's transaction pages may not be required if a balance summary is enough. Keep only the pages that matter with Extract PDF Pages.
  • Black out what isn't relevant, where the rules allow it. For a rental application, a landlord rarely needs your full account number or social security number. Use Redact PDF, which removes the text for real, and see how to redact a PDF properly. Do not redact documents for a visa or a government office unless they say you may.
  • Mark copies for a single use. For rental and other private applications, a watermark such as "Copy for the rental application to X, November 2026" makes a stolen copy less useful. See how to watermark a copy of your ID.

4. Get under the size limit

Use Compress PDF. Lossless keeps everything exactly as it is. For scans, Strong (150 DPI) is usually still clear enough for an official reader and makes files much smaller. Check that small print, such as a passport's machine-readable lines, is still legible before you send it.

5. Send it the right way

  • Use the official portal or the address given in the instructions, never one sent to you in an unexpected message.
  • If you email documents to a private landlord or agent, consider protecting the PDF with a password and sending the password by text message. Official portals usually cannot accept protected files.
  • Keep a copy of exactly what you sent, with the date.
