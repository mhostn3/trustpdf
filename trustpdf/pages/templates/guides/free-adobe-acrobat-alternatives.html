Adobe Acrobat Reader is free, but most of what people actually want to do with a PDF, such as merging, splitting, compressing, password protecting, redacting or recognising text in scans, needs a paid Acrobat plan. Adobe's website also offers some of these tasks as free online tools, which process your file on Adobe's servers. For occasional use, free tools cover nearly all of it. This guide lists the main free options for each task and is honest about where each one fits.

Three kinds of free PDF tools

  • Built into your system: macOS Preview, the iPhone and iPad Files app, and the PDF viewers in Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari. Nothing to install, but limited.
  • Free desktop programs: for example PDFsam Basic (open source, merge and split), LibreOffice Draw (edit PDF text and layout) and the command-line tools qpdf and pdfunite. They work offline but need installing, which is not always possible on a work or shared computer.
  • Web tools: nothing to install, they work on any device. Most upload your file to a server; a few, including TrustPDF, process it in your browser instead. Desktop, online or in-browser? compares the three approaches.

Free options by task

Reading, filling in forms and adding a signature

Acrobat Reader itself does this for free, and so do Preview on a Mac and most browsers' built-in PDF viewers for simple forms. In the browser, Fill PDF Form and Sign PDF work on any device, phones included.

Merging and splitting

On a Mac, Preview and Finder can merge; on Windows and Android there is nothing built in. PDFsam Basic is a solid desktop choice. In the browser, Merge PDF, Split PDF and Extract PDF Pages. Step-by-step instructions for each device are in how to merge PDFs on Mac, Windows, iPhone and Android.

Rotating, reordering and deleting pages

Preview can do all three. Elsewhere: Rotate PDF Pages, Reorder PDF Pages and Remove PDF Pages.

Compressing

Preview's Reduce File Size filter works, but it shrinks images hard and scans often come out blurry. Compress PDF offers a lossless option that keeps the file identical to the eye, plus stronger settings for scans.

Password protection

Preview can encrypt a PDF when you export it, and LibreOffice can add a password when it exports a document to PDF. To protect an existing PDF on any device, use Protect PDF (AES-256, like Acrobat). Unlock PDF removes a password you know.

Redaction

This is where free tools most often go wrong: drawing a black box in Preview or another viewer usually leaves the text underneath in the file. Redact PDF removes it, and how to redact a PDF explains how to check.

OCR and converting to Word

Microsoft Word can open a PDF and convert it to an editable document, with mixed results on complex layouts. For scans, OCR PDF adds searchable text in the browser, and PDF to Word converts to .docx.

Metadata

Acrobat Reader and Preview show a document's properties but cannot clear them all. The PDF Metadata Viewer and PDF Metadata Editor show and change the title and author, or strip everything including the hidden XMP block.

When paying for Acrobat still makes sense

Free tools are best for one-off tasks. If you edit the text of PDFs all day, need to compare document versions, prepare files for professional printing, or need certificate-based digital signatures that your organisation manages, a full PDF editor such as Acrobat Pro is worth considering. For merging an invoice, signing a lease or shrinking a scan, it is not necessary.
